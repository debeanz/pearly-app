Windows games. On your iPhone.

Pearly runs PC games inside one iOS app. Wine stands in for Windows, FEX translates the game's x86 code to ARM64 as it runs, and DXMT turns Direct3D into Metal. No jailbreak: you sideload it and let a debugger switch on JIT.

A research project, free and open source under GPL-3.0. Not released yet: expect rough edges and per-game quirks.

In the app

Built for playing, not for poking at Windows

Pearly opens on your games, not on a desktop. Here's what's there once you're in.

{{include:features.html}}

MetalFX upscaling

DirectX 11 games can draw fewer pixels and let MetalFX scale the picture up, by as much as 2×.

DirectX 11 mode

Many DirectX 12 games also ship a DirectX 11 mode. Pearly spots them and starts them in it, unless you switch it off.

Screen scaling

Fit, Fill, Stretch or whole-number scaling, and sharp pixels for pixel-art games, in Settings or for one game.

Key bar

Esc, Tab, Ctrl, Alt, the arrows and F1–F12 sit above the iPhone keyboard, for games that ask for them.

Your library

Favourites, search, sorting and the hours you've played, right on the Games tab.

Game Mode

Pearly tells iOS it's a game, so the system's Game Mode can switch on while you play.

Launchers and dialogs

A game's launcher, setup windows and message boxes appear over the game, so you can click through them.

.NET games

Wine Mono is built in, so games written for .NET, like Celeste, start without installing anything.

Questions

Good to know

All questions →
Does it need a jailbreak?

No. It's an ordinary sideloaded app. The one special requirement is JIT, which StikDebug provides by attaching as a debugger.

Which games work?

The compatibility list has what players have reported. Direct3D 11 games are the best bet: Unity games like Untitled Goose Game run, and so do 32-bit games such as Celeste and Undertale.

Can I download it?

Not yet. Pearly is still a research project and isn't released. You can follow every change in the build history and the progress reports.

Does it work on iPad?

Yes: iPhone and iPad on iOS 17 or later. It's developed and tested mostly on recent iPhones, and newer chips run games faster.
